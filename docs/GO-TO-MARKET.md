# Go-to-market: finding, converting and keeping customers (draft)

Draft of 2026-09-29; the owner's decisions of 2026-09-30 and 2026-10-01 are applied (section 14). Anything not marked decided is a proposal. Numbers carry a source in section 15. Anything marked *assumption* is a guess to replace with measurement.

## 1. The plan in ten lines

1. **Sell a finished website, not an AI tool.** Owners want to be found and called, not to build. AI is how we're fast and cheap, not the headline.
2. **The competitor is "Facebook is enough" and the freelancer**, not Wix. A small site from a Slovenian freelancer costs €250–1,500 plus €20–60/month; we price against that, not against Hostinger's €3.
3. **Brand: Izvesek** (recommended), the hanging sign above a craftsman's shop. `izvesek.si`, `.com` and `.eu` were free on 2026-09-29.
4. **First 15 customers by hand, in person, in one town** (decided, `sb-first-market`: restaurants, hairdressers and tradespeople; the town is still to be named by the owner): the owner sees their own site before their coffee is finished. They become design partners, case studies and the first real testimonials.
5. **Then the people owners already trust**: accountants (4,455 firms), OZS's 62 regional chambers and their trade sections, SPOT points, print shops and photographers.
6. **Then search**: a free "check your website" tool and honest Slovene content feeding the free preview. Google is 93 % of search in Slovenia.
7. **Conversion is the preview**: first look in ≤ 40 s, their own facts and photos, one short checklist from publishing, and a phone number on every screen.
8. **Retention is proof**: a monthly "42 people tapped Call" report, holiday-hours reminders, edits from the phone.
9. **Stand out where a global builder won't bother for a two-million market**: real Slovene, nothing invented, the domain in the owner's name, export any time, a person who answers the phone, one price with VAT included.
10. **No money before a legal entity** (taking payment unregistered counts as undeclared work). Billing is built first, then the business is registered before the first charge (decided, `sb-legal-entity`). A free waitlist with a reserved founding price until then; yearly invoices by bank transfer once the entity exists; billing and custom domains before CMS collections (decided, `sb-roadmap-order`).

## 2. Who buys, and when

**Market.** 121,255 s.p. in the business register (end 2025) and 223,010 micro enterprises (2024). About 19,700 new s.p. register each year (~54 a day) and ~18,500 close.

The ten core verticals hold ~28,000 businesses (2024):

| Restaurants and mobile food | 4,577 (of which restaurants and inns 2,493) |
| Car repair | 3,554 |

| Physical well-being | 1,351 |

It's ~52,000 counting all specialised construction (20,895) and retail (7,995). **450 paying customers, the rough point where this pays a founder (section 5), is 1.6 % of the core verticals.**

Nobody measures how many micro businesses have a website: Eurostat and SURS survey only firms with 10+ employees (83.9 % in Slovenia, 2025). That gap is a PR opportunity (section 8).

**Triggers.** Owners rarely wake up wanting a website; something triggers it. Channels should sit where the triggers happen:

| Trigger | Where we meet them |
|---|---|
| Starting a business (~54 new s.p. a day) | accountants, SPOT registration points, e-VEM/"how to start an s.p." content |
| Old site broken, the developer gone, "we can't change anything" | search ("prenova spletne strani"), the website checker, word of mouth |
| Hosting or domain renewal invoice | search, a January content and postal push |
| "I couldn't find you online"; no website on their Google Maps listing | in-person visits, Google Business Profile guide |
| Facebook page hacked or reach gone | content, community groups: "your site can't be taken from you" |
| Tourist season prep (Jan–Mar) | tourist boards, the tourist-farm association, Alpe-Adria fair (Feb) |
| A law or fine they heard about (cookies, ZEPT footer) | content, the checker's legal checks |

**Where to start.** Verticals where our site is visibly better than what they have, and where there's a reason to update often:

- **Restaurants and gostilne**: daily lunch menu (dnevna malica), price list, hours, tourists. Frequent updates give a reason to pay monthly. `Prices` and `Announcement` sections already exist.
- **Hairdressers and beauty**: price list, photos, booking link; many are on Instagram only. OZS has a hairdressers' section.
- **Tradespeople (electricians, plumbers, car repair)**: least online, most phone-driven; the click-to-call site is the whole product. OZS has sections for each.
- **Tourism next (tourist farms, private rooms)**: ~5,700 businesses, highest reason to pay (direct bookings instead of platform commission), but they need en/de locales first.

## 3. Positioning

**For us, not a slogan**: *For Slovenian tradespeople and small businesses who need to be found and called, Izvesek is a website you have today, change from your phone and own for good, for one flat price, without an agency.*

**Against the alternatives**

| What they do now | What they hear from us |
|---|---|
| Nothing, or a Facebook page | People search Google and Maps first. A page you own that Facebook can't lock or bury. |
| Freelancer or agency: €250–1,500 once, €20–60/month, changes billed | Live today, changes included, nobody to wait for |
| Telekom Slovenije "Spletna stran" (€11.90–50/month incl. VAT, setup up to €280 + VAT, 12-month contract) | No setup fee, no contract, cancel and take the site with you |
| Hostko, Neoserv, Domenca builders (€5–15/month, templates, AI text only) | Nothing to build: the site is finished, written and checked for you |
| Wix, Hostinger, Durable | Proper Slovene, no credits, no promo price that triples at renewal, a person to call |

**Proof points we can show today, all enforced in code**

- Written in real Slovene: dual and plural forms, `8.00`, `12,50 €`, `29. 9. 2026`.
- **Nothing invented.** No fake reviews, stock photos, "20 let izkušenj" or made-up prices. What we don't know stays a visible gap until the owner fills it. It's the answer to "AI makes things up", and a brand pillar, not a footnote.
- Built for the phone: call and directions one tap away on every page; fast on mobile data.
- **Yours**: the domain registered in your name, the whole site downloadable any time and working without us. A recurring complaint in Slovene forums is a developer who holds the domain or the site hostage.
- No cookie banner in front of their visitors unless they add a map or video; no tracking before consent.
- The provider information ZEPT requires is in the footer; privacy and accessibility statements are drafted for them to review.

**What not to lead with**: "AI" (to this audience in 2026 it reads as generic and untrustworthy; use it in press and SEO, not the hero), WCAG or GDPR jargon, "static HTML", Lighthouse scores. Translate each into an outcome: "stran se odpre v trenutku", "pokličejo vas z enim dotikom", "vse, kar zahteva zakon, je na svojem mestu".

**Claims must be provable** (ZVPot-1: advertising claims must be backed on request; comparisons within 8 days). Say specific, checkable things ("no cookies before consent", "passes automated accessibility checks"), not "GDPR compliant" or "fully accessible". Keep the check reports for every published site. Don't sell accessibility as a legal obligation: the EAA law (ZDPSI) exempts micro enterprises providing services and doesn't cover brochure sites without online ordering.

**Honest moat check.** Global builders' AI already writes passable Slovene, and Hostko sells an AI builder in Slovene for under €5/month. Language alone won't hold. What holds in a market this size: local trust and distribution (partners, in person, a Slovene phone line), vertical details (malica, cenik, ZEPT footer, tourist locales), the no-invention rule, and ownership.

## 5. Offer and pricing

Decided (`sb-pricing`, 2026-10-01); the numbers live in config (`plans.paid`). Billing isn't built yet and stays switched off in config until the legal entity exists (`sb-legal-entity`), so until then these are planned prices and the landing page says so.

- **One plan.** Full site, edits (chat, editor, phone), hosting, SSL, forms, export. No tier table; the decision is yes or no.
- **€15/month or €150/year, VAT included, the domain included on yearly.** Many s.p. aren't VAT-registered (threshold €60,000) and can't deduct it, and ZEPT requires prices on websites to include tax anyway. Same price whether or not we're VAT-registered.
- **Yearly by invoice and bank transfer (UPN QR).** Slovenian small businesses are used to a yearly hosting invoice. Bank transfers also avoid the fiscal-verification question that online card payments raise (section 9). Card/SEPA for monthly later, with Stripe.
- **Founding customers** (first 100): first year €99, then the normal price. Reserved on the waitlist before the entity exists (no payment), paid by invoice after.
- **"Uredimo namesto vas"** (we set it up with you): €79 one-off, optional. A 20-minute call, we fill the missing facts, connect the domain, set up the Google Business Profile. For owners who won't self-serve; during launch it's also the best customer research we can get.
- *Proposals, not part of the decision:* **free until you publish** (the preview and editing are free; payment starts at publish); **30 days money back**, and "cancel any time, take your site with you".

**Unit economics** (*assumptions* until billing runs). €150/year incl. VAT is €123 net. AI edits, domain, payment costs and a share of hosting ≈ €20–25/year per customer, so ~€100/year gross margin. Infrastructure (~€15–50/month) is covered by 5 customers. **~450 customers ≈ one founder's full-time income.** ~15 % of s.p. close every year, so even a perfect product loses customers. At a 2–3 year life a customer is worth ~€200–300 margin, so channels must acquire one for **≤ €60–80**.

## 7. Conversion

**Funnel to measure**: landing → preview started → preview shown → email confirmed → facts filled → published and paid → still paying at month 3 → referred someone.

**Targets to beat** (*assumptions*): landing → preview 15 %; preview → paid 5–8 % self-serve, 30 %+ after an in-person demo.

**The preview flow**

- Ask for the email first (to send the link), but show the preview **before** asking for confirmation. Confirmation unlocks saving, editing and publishing. Abuse control stays: per-IP limits and the daily spend cap. A preview costs €0.09; a gate before the wow loses more owners than it saves money. Decision: `sb-preview-gate`.
- **First look in ≤ 40 s** (Decision Inbox: `sb-preview-time`).
- **Start from what they already have**: photos of a printed menu, price list, flyer or business card, or their current website's URL. Owners hate typing on phones.
- **"Še 3 podatki do objave"**: required placeholders as a short checklist with progress, not as errors.
- **Publish → one price, VAT included, yearly option with the domain.** No plan table.
- **A human exit on every screen**: "Raje po telefonu? Pustite številko, pokličemo vas." For many owners this is the conversion path.

**Follow-up.** Messages about their own unfinished site are transactional. Tips and offers need an unticked opt-in box.

|---|---|

| day 1 | "Dodajte delovni čas in telefon, pa je stran pripravljena" |
| day 3 | a real case study from their vertical (opt-in) |

## 8. Retention, referral and PR

- **Monthly one-screen report**: visits, taps on Call and Directions, form enquiries. Counted server-side, no cookies or personal data, so no consent banner. It turns "why am I paying" into "42 people called from my site".
- **Timely nudges**: summer hours, holidays (1 May, August closures, Christmas–New Year), "Is your price list still right?" in January. One tap to update.
- **Edits from wherever they are**: chat now; email-to-edit next (cheap with Resend inbound); Viber or WhatsApp only after checking which one owners use (no reliable data) and what bot access costs.
- **Signature demo: photograph the chalkboard.** A gostilna photographs today's malica board; the site's menu updates (vision + a `Prices`/`Announcement` patch). Short, visual, shareable, and a daily reason to open the product.
- **Local clusters**: once three businesses in a town use it, the fourth hears their names (with permission).
- **"Spletne strani slovenskih malih podjetij 2026"**: run the checker on ~300 public small-business sites across verticals; publish aggregates only, no names. Official statistics skip firms under 10 employees, so this is new data for press, and its numbers become our pitch.
- **Language pride**: "Končno AI, ki zna dvojino." Correct dual and plural forms as a light campaign; Slovene media and social pick this subject up.

## 9. Legal limits on our own marketing and sales

Summary, not legal advice; sources in section 15.

- **Email**: prior consent for natural persons (ZEKom-2 Art. 226); legal persons need no prior consent but every message needs the sender and a working opt-out. Whether an s.p. is a natural person here is unresolved, so ask for consent. Generic `info@` addresses aren't personal data.
- **Post**: ordinary letters fall under GDPR; legitimate interest can work if the first letter says how to object (GDPR Art. 21). Where the address comes from matters: the contact details a business publishes on its own site are the safer source. IP RS has said register data should be used for the purpose it was collected (unverified), and ZVOP-2 Art. 93 excludes direct marketing from its public-source rule. Bulk AJPES extracts are paid.
- **Phone**: automated calls need consent; live calls to natural persons respect directory opt-outs; no national do-not-call register found.
- **Our claims**: must be provable on request; comparisons truthful and not disparaging (ZVPot-1 Art. 39, 42).
- **Selling**: payment before registering a business is "delo na črno" (ZPDZC-1). An s.p. registers free at SPOT or e-VEM with a start date from the next day; a d.o.o. needs €7,500 capital.
- **Invoices**: card payments, including online ones, can fall under fiscal verification of invoices (davčno potrjevanje); how FURS treats Stripe isn't clear. Bank-transfer invoices don't. Ask the accountant before building billing.
- **Our customers' sites**: ZEPT Art. 5 lists the mandatory provider details (name, seat, email, registration and tax numbers, permits and regulators, prices incl. tax). Our footer already covers most of these; permits/regulator for regulated professions (dentists, physio) should be checked.

## 10. Funding

Nothing currently pays for a small business's website:

- SPS "vavčer za digitalni marketing" (60 %, websites eligible) ran 2019–2022 and isn't in the current voucher list.
- P4D digital transformation (websites eligible up to €5,000) closed on 7. 5. 2025, needs 5+ employees; no 2026 call found.
- No municipal website subsidies found; the old ZRSZ self-employment subsidy isn't running.

So: don't build pricing around vouchers. Watch SPS and the Ministry for Digital Transformation for new calls, and keep a voucher-shaped package ready (e.g. 3 years prepaid + setup + multilingual) in case one opens.
