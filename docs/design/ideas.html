Design system proposal · v0 · draft for discussion

Quiet chrome,
loud content.

Two things need a design system: the product itself (login, intake, editor, publish) and the websites it generates. Today the product looks like a 2014 admin panel, and the generated sites all share one skeleton. This page proposes tokens, components and screens for the product, a way to raise the ceiling of the generated sites, and a hard list of AI-site give-aways we refuse to produce.

The good parts to keep

02

What Base44 gets right, and what we don't copy

Borrow the posture

  • One canvas. A warm off-white page with a faint dot grid, not pure white, not grey. Everything sits on it; cards are rare.
  • One huge headline. A tight grotesk at 72 px+ with negative tracking does the branding. No illustration needed.
  • One accent. An orange dot in the logo and almost nowhere else. Buttons are ink-black.
  • The input is the hero. The first thing you see is the box where you type. The product demonstrates itself.
  • Big preview, thin chrome. Inside the app the generated thing fills the screen; controls are small and at the edges.
  • Confidence in copy. Short, declarative, no hedging.

Leave the surface

  • Pill buttons, em-dashes in every second sentence, "Seamlessly collaborate", "Beautiful by default", a "1 week to launch / $1M ARR" stat row, a founder testimonial. Several of these are on the give-away list below.
  • Base44 sells to builders worldwide. We sell to a hairdresser in Celje who manages the site from a phone. Our register is plainer, our Slovene is natural, and our facts are the client's facts.
  • We don't need a dark mode, a video generator or a 3-D hero. We need the intake to feel effortless and the editor to feel safe.

Rule of thumb: copy the restraint, not the tricks.

03

Principles

1. Quiet chrome, loud content

The dashboard's job is to show the client's site. UI is ink on a cool blue-white canvas, hairline borders, no cards inside cards. The preview is the only thing with a shadow.

2. One canvas, one ink, one accent

Warm neutral canvas, near-black ink, a single accent used for the primary action and state, never for decoration. Try the four accents in the top bar.

3. Type does the talking

A display face with character (Bricolage Grotesque) for headings, a clean humanist sans (Figtree) for UI. Big sizes, tight leading, generous margins. No icons where a word will do.

4. Borders, not shadows

Structure comes from 1 px lines and background steps. Exactly one shadow token, reserved for things that float: the prompt box, the phone frame, the chat drawer, toasts.

5. Phone first, on both sides

Owners manage the site from a phone. Every product screen is designed at 360 px first: bottom sheets, 44 px targets, one primary action per screen.

6. Nothing generic

If a pattern is on the "don't use" list, the component can't produce it. Variety is enforced by structure (hero families, header families), not by hoping the model picks differently.

Space, radius, elevation, motion

Space

4-px grid. Component padding 12/16, section gaps 24/32, page gutters 20 (360 px) and 32+. Max content width 1180.

Radius

4 (tags, small controls), 8 (buttons, inputs, cards), 12 (large containers). No pills. Circles only for status dots and avatars.

Elevation

Hairline borders everywhere. One shadow token, for floating elements only. No hover-lift, no glow.

Motion

150 ms ease-out on colour and background; 200 ms on sheets and drawers. No entrance animations, no parallax. Respects reduced motion.

Focus and targets

2 px ink outline with 2 px offset. Every control at least 44 px on touch, 34 px in dense toolbars on desktop only.

Language

UI in Slovene, short and declarative: "Ustvari", "Objavi", "Prenesi". Numbers and dates in Slovene format. No exclamation marks.

07

Generated sites: raise the ceiling

The directions are fine. The skeleton under them is the problem. Proposal: give each direction its own opening move, header family and section rhythm, so two sites from different directions differ in structure, not only in font.

Same on 9 of 9 sites todayProposal
Hero = eyebrow, H1, paragraph, two stacked full-width buttons, photo belowFour hero families, each direction gets one or two: facts-first (a dark fact band with phone, hours and address directly under a short headline), photo-first (photo at the very top, headline over the bottom third or just under it), type-only (one big headline, one button, phone as an underlined text link), split (what we have, kept for directions where it fits). The second button becomes a text link in most families.
Header = logo left, outlined "Meni" button rightThree header families: bar (logo, phone icon-button, menu), stacked (centred wordmark, thin rule, menu below it on desktop), split-cta (logo, menu, filled call button). Menu is a word without a box in two of the three.
Every section is a full-width block with the same paddingRhythm per direction is already in the spec (flat, alternate, inverse-accents) but visually weak. Make it strong: inverse bands full-bleed, alternate sections with a real background step, flat sections separated by hairlines and bigger type.
Photos in the same 4:3 boxThe eight imagery treatments exist (framed, offset-block, arched, duotone, full-bleed …). Use them more aggressively and make the hero photo ratio a direction property: 5:4 offset for Warm Craft, 16:9 full-bleed for Alpine, framed portrait for Dark Elegant.
Headline sizes cluster around 32 px on mobileAllow the display size to go to 40–44 px on 360 px for short headlines (the content stage knows the length), and 64–80 px on desktop. Tight leading (1.02–1.08).
Two buttons, both 48 px full widthOne primary. The second action is a text link with an underline, or the phone number itself. Two identical buttons is a give-away.

Three "after" heroes at 360 px

Static HTML mocks using the directions' own fonts and fallback colours. Same rules: client photos only, no invented facts, contrast enforced, call bar kept.

08

Don't use this: the AI-site give-aways

Compiled from external opinion, not from our own guesses: 16 web searches, about 25 pages read, including a scored survey of 500 Show HN landing pages, designers' "spot the slop" guides, and Reddit-derived playbooks. Ranked by how many distinct sources named the tell. Each item is marked with what happens to it here.

code impossible by construction (token range, schema, stylesheet test, no JS) critique caught by the screenshot critique pass or a copy check new not covered yet, becomes a rule in this overhaul watch we are close to it, needs care

Caveats from the research: no first-hand Reddit or Hacker News thread could be opened, so those tells arrive second-hand. Several sources sell "anti-slop" tools and have an incentive to call defaults slop. The share figures come from one survey of 500 pages. None of the sources cover Slovene copy; the Slovene word list further down is our own inference.

Visual and structural tells

1 · Purple, indigo or violet as the primary, and purple-to-blue gradients16 sourcescodeThe single most-named tell, traced to Tailwind's indigo defaults. Gradients fail our stylesheet test; the primary comes from the client's logo; no fallback palette is purple. Add: when the logo yields a hue in 250–290°, the design stage must justify it or fall back.
2 · Three identical feature cards with icons11critiqueIcon, two-word title, one sentence, equal length, in a row of three. Already on the PRODUCT.md list; the components have no icon-card variant. Keep it that way when the services and highlights sections get new variants.
3 · Inter, Roboto or Geist everywhere10code"Change the font first" is the most-cited fix. Generated sites use ten curated pairs; only one uses Inter. The product UI moves from system-ui to Bricolage Grotesque and Figtree.
4 · Everything centred: centred hero, centred CTA, centred sections9code23.5% of surveyed pages. A stylesheet test rejects centred section defaults; only listed variants (the stacked wordmark, a CTA band) may centre.
5 · Category-level copy that fits any competitor9critiqueThe "cover the logo" test: if the page still makes sense for a rival, it is generic. Our copy is built from the client's facts, and filler phrases are rejected in code. Add the cover-the-logo question to the critique prompt.
6 · One radius on everything, pill buttons, rounded-2xl cards7codeRadius token capped at 12 px, pills rejected by the stylesheet test. Product UI uses three radii by role (4, 8, 12).
7 · Gradients on backgrounds, titles and borders7code28% of surveyed pages. Any gradient function in the stylesheet fails the test.
8 · Dark mode with neon glow: purple or cyan accent, radial halos, glowing shadows7codeOnly one shadow token exists, so glows are impossible. Dark Elegant uses warm near-black with brass, not neon.
9 · Coloured left or top border on cards6newCalled "almost as reliable a sign as em-dashes". Not banned yet. Add to the stylesheet test: no accent-coloured single-side borders on cards or list items.
10 · Emoji as icons6codeAny pictograph in generated copy is rejected.
11 · Soft grey shadows on floating cards, or border + shadow + tint stacked on one card5codeShadow is "none" or one subtle token per site. Product UI: borders only, one shadow for floating elements.
12 · A badge or pill above the H1 ("New", "Live", "AI-powered")5newOur heroes have a plain-text eyebrow, never a pill. Make it explicit in the component contract, and make the eyebrow optional per hero family so it stops appearing on 9 of 9 sites.
13 · Glassmorphism, frosted panels5code17.1% of surveyed pages. backdrop-filter fails the stylesheet test.
14 · Cream or beige page, serif headings, rust or sage accent5watchThe newer "tasteful" default that anti-purple advice produced; a Reddit comment calls beige-and-green alone a giveaway. Cream backgrounds are banned here, but Warm Craft (Fraunces, terracotta, pale sage surface) sits next to this tell. Keep the white page, let the offset photo block and layout carry the character, and take the accent from the logo before reaching for terracotta.
15 · ALL-CAPS tracked-out eyebrow labels above every heading5newEyebrows stay sentence case in every direction. Uppercase is a heading-only token, allowed in Bold Local and Industrial. Cap letter-spacing at 0.04 em (today 0.08).
16 · Hover-lift and scroll animations on every card5codeGenerated sites ship no animation JS and nothing depends on hover. Product UI: 150 ms colour transitions only, no entrance animations.
17 · Uniform padding, radius and card height; flat type hierarchy5newThis is the sameness on our own contact sheet. Fixed by section 07: hero and header families, bigger display sizes, section rhythm that is visible.
18 · Italic serif accent word inside a sans headline4codeItalic headings fail the stylesheet test.
19 · Gradient blob or spotlight behind the hero4codeCovered by the gradient ban.
20 · Unmodified shadcn/ui: slate cards, stock border-radius-shadow4code23.5% of surveyed pages. We render with our own component library, no UI kit.
21 · Invented statistics: "10,000+ users", "47%", "3×", metric rows4codeNo component has a stats prop. Facts come only from the client's input.
22 · Low-contrast grey body text4code4.5:1 enforced for every text and background pair, including muted text.
23 · Cookie-cutter section order: hero, logos, features, testimonial, pricing, FAQ, CTA4newLogos and testimonials can't exist here. Add: each direction carries a preferred section order for its business types, and the critique flags the generic order.
24 · Mad-lib headlines: "The X for modern Y", "Scale without limits", "Dobrodošli"3critique"Dobrodošli" and "Welcome to" are rejected in code. Add Slovene equivalents of the mad-lib forms (see copy list).
25 · Numbered "01 / 02 / 03" labels and 1-2-3 step rows3codeNumbered labels are rejected; the steps component carries order in the list, draws no numbers.
26 · Stock photos, generic illustrations, unrelated team photos3codeClient photos only. With few photos the layout becomes typography-led.
27 · No empty, loading or error states3newProduct UI: every list has an empty state, generation has the stage list, every failure has a message with a next action. Part of the component set above.
28 · Wide letter-spacing, also on body text3codeTracking is a heading-only token; tighten its ceiling to 0.04 em.
29 · Placeholder people and brands: John Doe, Sarah Johnson, Acme2codeNames and prices are placeholders until the client fills them; unfilled placeholders block publishing.
30 · "Trusted by" logo bar, invented testimonials2codeNo such components exist.
31 · Pulsing status dot, "Live" pill on things that aren't live2newProduct UI: status dots are static; "Objavljeno" appears only when a version is actually published.
32 · Marquee or ticker bars2codeNo animation JS in generated sites.
33 · Nested cards2newProduct UI principle 1. For generated sites: a section on a surface tone never contains bordered cards.
34 · Decorative dot or grid backgrounds2newBase44 uses one; we don't. Not in generated sites, not in the product.
35 · Generic dashboard mockup or blob illustration in the hero2codeClient photos only.
36 · Chevron arrow appended to every button, icons in coloured circles1+1newButtons are words. No decorative arrows, no icon-in-circle pattern in any component.
37 · Pure #000 background with #fff text1newDark Elegant already uses #14110f. Add a rule: page backgrounds stay off-black and off-white (lightness bounds in the design rules).
38 · Two identical full-width buttons stacked under every headlineoursnewOur own finding from the contact sheet, not from the sources. One primary action per hero; the second is a text link or the phone number.

Two things the sources cleared: bento grids (0.1% of complaints, often defended) and aurora or mesh backgrounds when chosen deliberately. One source's framing is the useful one: a tell is an unspecified default, not a banned colour. Cobalt chosen on purpose is fine; indigo because the model didn't decide is not.

Copy tells

From the sources (English)

  • Em-dashes, named as the canonical text-side tell.
  • Forced contrast as a slogan: "Not X. Y."
  • Rule-of-three fragments: "Save time. Work smarter. Scale faster."
  • Unfalsifiable, equal-length claims nobody can check.
  • Vocabulary: seamless, streamline, unlock, unleash, unveil, supercharge, empower, elevate, leverage, robust, world-class, cutting-edge, game-changer, transformative, "in today's fast-paced world", "at the forefront", firstly/secondly/lastly.
  • Timid corporate register and headlines that read like a template with the nouns swapped.
  • Caveat from the sources: skilled humans use some of these too; word lists produce false positives. Use them as a check, not as the whole judgement.

Slovene rules to add new

Our inference: the structural tells are language-independent; the words are calques of the English list. Extend packages/spec/src/banned.ts.

  • No em-dash (U+2014) anywhere in generated copy. Slovene uses the spaced en-dash (pomišljaj, " – ") or a comma. One regex, code-enforced.
  • No "Ne X. Y." contrast slogans and no three-fragment headline runs ("Hitro. Zanesljivo. Domače.").
  • Filler to add: brezhibno, celovit pristop, dvignite/povzdignite na višjo raven, odklenite, opolnomočimo, inovativne rešitve, vrhunski, po meri vaših potreb, z vami na vsakem koraku, v današnjem hitrem tempu, sanje se uresničijo, izkusite.
  • Mad-lib headline shapes: "Vaš X za Y", "Več kot le X", "X na višji ravni", "Kjer se X sreča z Y".
  • Every headline must contain a fact the client gave (place, product, year, method, person). The critique already asks for this in spirit; make it a check: at least one noun from the brief's facts.
  • No exclamation marks in generated copy or product UI.

What the sources say reads as human-made

Show the real thing

Real photos of the shop, the workshop, the people. One specific claim with a number, a name or a date. We already do this by construction; the design has to give the photo and the fact the space they deserve.

Choose, don't default

One deliberately chosen accent per viewport, off-black not black, a typeface with an opinion, two to four effects per page and the rest plain.

Vary on purpose

Bigger jumps in the type scale, an off-centre hero, a non-uniform grid, primary elements visibly larger than secondary ones, sections ordered by the strongest argument rather than by template.

09

Plan

Decisions for you

  1. Accent: green, orange, blue or ink-only (top bar toggle).
  2. Display face: Bricolage Grotesque (this page) or Inter Tight (safer).
  3. Product name for the wordmark. "Graditelj strani" is a description, not a name. Needed before the login screen is worth designing.
  4. Order: product UI first (small, 1 PR, replaces two CSS strings), then generated-site hero and header families (spec change, version bump, migration, eval), then the editor redesign as part of phase 2 rather than before it.
  5. Approve the additions to the banned-patterns list in docs/PRODUCT.md from section 08.

What each step ships with

  • Product UI: tokens as CSS variables in one shared stylesheet for apps/web, components rewritten in place, no new framework. Check: screenshots at 360 and 1280, axe on every page, the existing smoke test.
  • Site heroes and headers: new variants in packages/spec sections and packages/components, direction layout preferences updated, the eval contact sheet as the check (no two directions may share a hero family and header family).
  • Give-aways: each new item either becomes a grep in the stylesheet test, a schema constraint, or a line in the critique prompt. The list itself lands in PRODUCT.md.
  • Editor: the 360-px bottom-sheet layout, with the Puck decision from phase 2.
